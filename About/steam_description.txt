[h1]Regions and Societies — Realistic Planets 2 Edition[/h1]
[b][url=https://github.com/Regions-and-societies/Core-MMF/wiki]Official Wiki and Documentation[/url][/b]
[b]Overhauls the world map with dynamic geographic province generation, world map overlays, and territory borders — built for Realistic Planets 2.[/b]

This is the Realistic Planets 2 build of Regions and Societies. It is identical to the standard mod except that it runs on the map-mode framework bundled with Realistic Planets 2 instead of the standalone Map Mode Framework. [b]Use this edition if you play with Realistic Planets 2; use the standard edition otherwise.[/b] Do not run both editions, or both frameworks, at once.

Regions and Societies is the continuation of Regions and Territories under a new identity. It divides the globe into dynamic geographic provinces with defined borders, population densities, custom road linkages, and procedurally placed ruins.

---

[h2]Best on a New Colony - Existing Saves Supported in Compatibility Mode[/h2]
[b]A world generated with the mod installed gets everything. A world already in progress is adopted in Compatibility Mode.[/b]

Regions and Societies does most of its work during world generation. It draws the province boundaries and then places faction settlements according to them, respecting region occupancy limits, border buffers, and sequential expansion.

Added to a world that already exists, it adopts that world in Compatibility Mode. Provinces are still drawn and territory is still owned and shown, but placement stands down: the mod will not decide where settlements and outposts may be built, because that world was settled long before it arrived. Vanilla and other mods keep control of placement, and more than one settlement may share a province.

The mode is decided once per save and recorded in it. You can see and change it under [b]Strict territorial ownership[/b] in the mod settings while that save is loaded - though switching an in-progress world to strict starts applying rules to settlements that were never placed under them.

[b]A new colony is still the recommended way to play.[/b] Compatibility mode makes an existing save usable, not equivalent - geography-driven placement, occupancy limits, border buffers and sequential expansion are the heart of the mod, and only a world generated with it installed gets them.

Removing it from an in-progress save logs errors about missing province data on next load; the save usually still opens, but this is not a supported path.

[h2]Key Features[/h2]
[list]
[*] [b]Geographic Domain Mapping:[/b] Splits the world into dynamic provinces with clear, terrain-aware boundaries.
[*] [b]Territorial Ownership:[/b] Factions own the ground they actually hold - a four-tier ladder from exclusive hold to loose claim, shaded on the map, with coastlines and mountains reading as real borders.
[*] [b]Geography-Driven Faction Placement:[/b] Nations settle as contiguous territory, weighing established claim against resources and shying away from a rival's ground - one settlement per region, with intrusions costing goodwill, and squaring their domains off rather than spidering.
[*] [b]Regional Demographics (new in 0.2.0):[/b] Every region carries a deterministic demographic profile across seven axes - age structure, sex ratio, xenotype castes, ideoligions, wealth, education and employment - derived from the factions pressing on it, their tech and beliefs, and the land itself. Nothing is stored in your save.
[*] [b]Demographic Map Overlays:[/b] Seven world-map overlays, one per axis, alongside faction territory and the population-density heatmap - all running on Realistic Planets 2's bundled map-mode framework. The expanded region panel shows the full breakdown.
[*] [b]One-Click Overlay Toggles:[/b] Region borders and the Territories / Dwellings map modes each get their own toggle icon in the world-view controls - no digging through menus.
[*] [b]Hooks for Other Mods:[/b] War and drafting mods can skew a region's sex ratio (drafts in progress, combat losses with generational recovery); expansion mods can rank territory with the same compactness metric core uses.
[*] [b]Procedural Trade Infrastructure:[/b] Links settlements with trade roads and places historical ruins across the world map.
[/list]

[h2]Changelog (v0.2.2) - Worldgen fixes and performance[/h2]
[list]
[*] [b]Fixed: black world on generate.[/b] Faction generation built each faction's ideoligion without the faction context and planet layer vanilla supplies, and classic (no-expansion) ideoligion role naming crashed world generation - the planet never rendered. Generation now mirrors vanilla's own call exactly, and a per-faction guard degrades any future faction failure to a logged skip instead of a dead world.
[*] [b]The player always has somewhere to land.[/b] At small worlds or low coverage, NPC placement could claim every settleable province and the starting-site chooser found no valid tile. Placement now always leaves at least one settleable land province unclaimed.
[*] [b]Worldgen performance at scale.[/b] Terrain features are read from the world grid once instead of once per faction, and the tribal betweenness bonus no longer rebuilds its industrial-base map per candidate province - large planets (high planet scale, 100% coverage) generate dramatically faster.
[/list]

[h2]Changelog (v0.2.1) - Hotfix[/h2]
[list]
[*] Fixed: the in-game debug actions menu (dev mode) failed to open while the mod was installed. A debug action carried a parameter, which RimWorld cannot bind, so building the menu threw and it never opened. Removed the offending action; the menu now opens normally. No gameplay or save changes.
[/list]

[h2]Changelog (v0.2.0) - Regional Demographics[/h2]
[list]
[*] [b]Seven demographic axes per region[/b], each with a map overlay, region-panel breakdown and public endpoint: age structure (tech-level pyramids, natalist memes, longevity genes), sex ratio, xenotype castes, ideoligion shares with neighbour belief-similarity, wealth/SES tiers (terrain richness and trade roads count), education tiers, and an occupation mix with employment rate.
[*] [b]Derived, never stored:[/b] everything is computed on read from the world seed - deterministic on every machine, nothing added to the save file.
[*] [b]Honest DLC degradation:[/b] without Biotech the xenotype overlay says "all Baseliner"; without Ideology every region is secular - no flat map pretending to be data.
[*] [b]DemographicHooks API:[/b] companion mods report drafts in progress (transient sex-ratio skew) or combat losses (a generational scar recovering over a configurable number of years, default 15, slider provided).
[*] [b]Territory compactness:[/b] faction domains square off instead of spidering, tunable with a new slider; expansion mods can rank candidate provinces with the same public metric.
[*] [b]Dwellings readout:[/b] the population tooltip answers for every habitable tile (zero included) as a compass block of the tile and its neighbours.
[/list]

[h2]Compatibility Matrix[/h2]
[list]
[*] [b]Royalty DLC:[/b] Compatible
[*] [b]Ideology DLC:[/b] Compatible
[*] [b]Biotech DLC:[/b] Compatible
[*] [b]Anomaly DLC:[/b] Compatible
[*] [b]Realistic Planets 2:[/b] Required - it supplies the map-mode framework this edition runs on.
[*] [b]Map Mode Framework (standalone) and the standard Regions and Societies edition:[/b] Incompatible with this edition by design - all three declare each other incompatible because only one framework may load. Use the standard edition with Map Mode Framework instead.
[*] [b]Empire, World Domination, Vanilla Factions Expanded, Vanilla Outposts Expanded:[/b] Supported through dedicated companion compatibility patches, released separately. The patches bind whichever edition is present. Core alone classifies vanilla world objects.
[*] [b]Existing saves:[/b] Supported in Compatibility Mode - provinces are generated and territory is drawn, but placement rules stand down and stay with vanilla or whichever mod owns them. A new colony still gets the full experience.
[/list]
